📍 Start Here

You're staying in the Vanderbilt/Midtown pocket, which is genuinely one of the best places to base yourself. Centennial Park is a walk away, you're 10–15 minutes from almost everything, and both of the food scenes you care about are a straight shot down one road.

☀️ The single most important thing: it's August. Highs around 88–92°F, humidity around 70%, and a heat index that regularly hits 100°F+. This changes how you plan a day — not whether you go outside. Hike before 10am. Do museums, long lunches, and shops from noon to 4. Go back outside after 5. Afternoon thunderstorms pop up and pass; they're usually not a day-ender.
🚗 You'll want a car. Nashville is spread out and transit is thin. Uber/Lyft works fine downtown, but every hike on this list and most of the best food needs wheels. Parking is easy and mostly free everywhere except downtown/The Gulch.

The three roads that matter

Nolensville Pike

~15 min south
Your food street. Full stop.

This is Nashville's immigrant corridor — Kurdish, Mexican, Somali, Vietnamese, Salvadoran — stacked in strip malls for about five miles. It's where both of your favorite cuisines live, and it is dramatically better than anything downtown. Unglamorous buildings, outstanding food.

Highway 100 / Belle Meade

~15 min southwest
Your nature road.

Runs you straight out to Percy Warner Park, Cheekwood, and on toward the Harpeth River. Leafy, rolling, and quick to get to.

Gallatin Pike / East Nashville

~15 min east
Where locals actually go out.

Independent restaurants, dive bars, record shops, coffee. If you want a night out that isn't a bachelorette party, come here instead of Broadway.

🥾 Hiking & Nature

Organized by how far you have to drive. Middle Tennessee is limestone-and-hardwood country — shaded ridges, hollows, creeks, and cedar glades. The waterfalls are worth the extra driving.

Right out the door — under 20 minutes

Radnor Lake State Park

15 min
1160 Otter Creek Rd · ~6 miles of trail · easy to moderate ★ TOP PICK

The best nature inside the city, and it's not close. A quiet lake ringed by forest, 200+ bird species, and near-guaranteed deer, turtles, herons, and often owls. No running, no bikes, no dogs on the natural trails — which is exactly why it stays peaceful.

Do this: Lake Trail out, Ganier Ridge back (~3.5 mi) for a little climb and an overlook. Go at 7am. Trails open 6am; the lots are small and fill fast, and morning is when the wildlife is out. If the main lot is full, people park at the church across the street.

Percy Warner Park — Mossy Ridge & Warner Woods

15 min
Deep Well trailhead off Hwy 100 · 2.5 mi or 4.5 mi loops · moderate

3,100 acres of oak-hickory forest with real elevation — this is where Nashville trains for things. Warner Woods (2.5 mi, white blaze) is the shorter loop; Mossy Ridge (4.5 mi, red blaze) rolls through hollows, past springs and small seasonal waterfalls, and through open meadows. Dogs allowed on leash.

Bonus: Drive up to the Allée & Belle Meade Stone Steps at the Belle Meade Blvd entrance — a grand limestone staircase up to a hilltop view. Locals run these. You can just walk them and enjoy the view.

Edwin Warner Park + Nature Center

15 min
Across Hwy 100 from Percy Warner · easy

Percy's gentler twin. Flatter trails, a small nature center with exhibits and staff who'll tell you what's blooming or nesting, and the Harpeth Woods Trail (2.5 mi) through an old quarry. Good for a low-effort morning or an evening walk.

Shelby Bottoms Greenway

15 min east
960 acres · 5+ mi paved, 5+ mi primitive · flat

Bottomland forest and wetland along three miles of the Cumberland River. Paved and flat, so it's the easy pick when it's hot or you just want to move. Great birding, and a pedestrian bridge connects across the river. Pair it with breakfast or tacos in East Nashville.

Half-day — 30 to 50 minutes out

Narrows of the Harpeth

35 min west
Harpeth River State Park · ~0.8 mi of trails · short but steep-ish ★ TOP PICK

Small park, huge payoff. The Bluff Trail climbs to Table Top Rock, a limestone ledge with the best river-gorge view near Nashville — the most photographed spot in the park. The Tunnel Trail takes you to the Montgomery Bell Tunnel, a 290-foot waterway hand-dug through solid rock in 1818 to power an iron forge. It's a National Historic Landmark and genuinely strange to stand in front of.

Best combo: Hike the bluff in the morning, then rent a kayak and float the Harpeth in the afternoon (see Water, below). Trails are short — you can do all three in about an hour.

Beaman Park

30 min northwest
1,500 acres · 14+ mi of trail · moderate

The most genuinely remote-feeling park still inside Davidson County. Deep hollows, creek crossings, wildflowers, very few people. The Sedge Hill + Henry Hollow combo (~3.2 mi) from the nature center is the standard loop. Bring bug spray — it's real woods.

Long Hunter State Park

30 min east
On Percy Priest Lake · easy to strenuous

Two very different options. Couchville Lake Trail is a flat, paved 2-mile loop around a lake — easy and pretty, with a boardwalk section. The Volunteer Trail runs 5+ miles one way along the shore for something longer. There's also a rare cedar glade ecosystem here — thin-soil limestone barrens with wildflowers that grow almost nowhere else on earth. Swimming at Bryant Grove.

Montgomery Bell State Park

45 min west
Three lakes · ~19 mi of trail

Rolling forest around three lakes, an 11.7-mile overnight loop if you want a real day of it, and shorter lake loops if you don't. Quieter than the in-town parks and shaded almost the whole way.

Old Stone Fort State Archaeological Park

70 min southeast
Manchester, TN · 1.25 mi loop + spur trails · easy

A 2,000-year-old Native American ceremonial earthwork built on a bluff between two rivers — and the loop trail passes something like five waterfalls and cascades. Wildly underrated. Shaded, flat, and about as much waterfall-per-mile as you'll find.

Worth the day trip — waterfalls

Cummins Falls State Park

1 hr 30 min east
75-ft falls into an emerald swimming hole ★ IF YOU DO ONE

The best swimming-hole waterfall in Tennessee. A 1.5-mile hike down, involving stream crossings and scrambling over wet limestone, drops you at a wide plunge pool at the base of the falls.

⚠️ You must buy a gorge permit in advance. $7 online only, at reserve.tnstateparks.com, capped at ~200 per day, and they sell out. You'll watch a short safety video. Buy it the moment you know your date. Gorge access closes on any rain in the watershed (flash flood risk — this is not a formality), and there are no refunds or rain checks. The overlook and upper trails don't need a permit, so the trip isn't wasted if the gorge closes. Wear shoes you can soak — water shoes or old sneakers, not flip-flops.

Rock Island State Park

1 hr 40 min east
Great Falls, Caney Fork Gorge, sand beach

Water everywhere. The Caney Fork Gorge below the dam is a moonscape of limestone shelves with waterfalls pouring straight out of the cliff face, plus rock-hopping, swimming, and natural pools (the "Cold Hole" and "Warm Hole"). There's also a real sand beach with a bathhouse on Center Hill Lake.

Heads up: water levels are controlled by dam releases and can rise fast. Obey the sirens and posted warnings, and stay out of the gorge when they're running.

Fall Creek Falls State Park

2 hr east
256-ft falls — tallest free-fall waterfall east of the Mississippi

The big one. If you want one full day that feels like a different state, this is it — four major waterfalls, a gorge, suspension bridges, and overlooks. The Woodland Trail (0.9 mi) plus the Gorge Overlook spur (0.2 mi) is the best effort-to-view ratio; the descent to the base of Fall Creek Falls is short but steep and rocky. It's a long drive, so leave early and make a day of it.

Burgess Falls State Park

1 hr 30 min east
Four waterfalls on one 1.5-mi river trail

The River Trail passes three waterfalls before reaching an observation deck over the 136-foot main falls. Gorgeous and compact.

Two things to know: there is currently no trail access to the base of the falls — the base is reachable only by boat/kayak from Cane Hollow. And a new visitor center is under construction, so the upper lot is closed and parking is very limited. Go early or pair it with Cummins Falls (they're 25 min apart).

Short Springs State Natural Area

80 min southeast
Tullahoma · under 2 mi round trip

A quiet, low-key alternative: Machine Falls drops ~60 feet into a mossy amphitheater at the end of a short shaded trail. Almost never crowded compared to the state parks.

🛶 On the Water

In August this is arguably the best way to spend an outdoor afternoon — you're in the shade of the river bluffs and in the water at the same time.

Float the Harpeth River

20–35 min west
Canoe, kayak, tube rentals with shuttle ★ TOP PICK

The Harpeth is a slow, clear, Class I river — the third most biologically diverse river in the country — winding through limestone bluffs. Outfitters run you upstream and you paddle back down. Trips run roughly 2 to 5 hours depending on the put-in. Herons, turtles, bluffs, and swimming holes the whole way.

Three outfitters: Broken Paddle Outfitters (8547 US-70, Bellevue — closest to you, ~15 min from downtown, 7 days a week, book at least 12 hours ahead), Foggy Bottom Canoe (Kingston Springs, the classic), and Sunshine's Adventures. Book ahead on weekends. Bring water shoes and a dry bag for your phone.

Percy Priest Lake — swimming

25 min east
Anderson Road, Cook, Hamilton Creek, Bryant Grove

The big lake on Nashville's east side, warmest in July and August. Anderson Road Day Use has the best sandy swim beach with roped-off zones, restrooms, and a playground ($5/vehicle, no pets, can close when full — go early on weekends). Hamilton Creek is quieter with a pebbled beach and easy kayak/paddleboard launching. Bryant Grove is inside Long Hunter State Park, so you can hike and swim on the same trip.

🥙 Middle Eastern

Here's the thing nobody tells you about Nashville: it's home to the largest Kurdish community in North America — over 15,000 people, here since 1976. The neighborhood off Nolensville Pike is called Little Kurdistan, and it is the reason you're going to eat better here than you expect.

Kurdish & Turkish — Little Kurdistan

Edessa Restaurant

15 min
3802 Nolensville Pike · Kurdish & Turkish · roughly 11am–8:30/9pm daily ★ START HERE

The crown jewel of the strip, opened in 2018 by three Kurdish men who emigrated from Turkey. Unassuming strip mall on the outside; inside there are hunks of meat hanging on a rack and pillowy bubbles of lavash thrown straight from the oven onto your table. This is the one to build a meal around.

Order: the mixed grill kabob (easily feeds two), plus hummus, ezme, and whatever lavash comes out of the oven. Turkish coffee and baklava to finish. If you end up with a group, the "kabob festival" spread is the move.

House of Kabob

15 min
One of Nashville's oldest Kurdish restaurants

The old guard — around long before anyone was writing articles about Little Kurdistan. Straightforward, generous, consistently good kabobs and stews. Go here if Edessa has a wait.

Newroz Market & House of Shawarma

15 min
Market + kitchen in the back ★ SLEEPER PICK

"Newroz" is Kurdish for new day. Front of house is a genuinely great pantry market — olive oils, syrups, spices, nuts, rice, pulses, halal meat, fresh bread. Tucked in the back is a kitchen carving shawarma straight off the spit into house-baked bread. This is the kind of place you'd never find on your own.

Order: a shawarma wrap at the back counter, then shop the aisles while you eat it.

Azadi International Food Market & Bakery

15 min
Market + bakery, near Elysian Fields

Come for the bakery counter. Fresh flatbread, pastries, and baklava, plus a full international grocery. Great place to grab breakfast to take on a hike.

Sulav International Market

15 min
Halal meats, fresh bread, shawarma, homemade baklava

Third of the market trio. The homemade baklava is the reason to stop.

Reebar (Jerusalem Reebar)

15 min
Kurdish home cooking

Less kabob-focused, more the stews and rice dishes you'd actually eat at home. Worth it if you want something beyond grilled meat.

Elsewhere in the city

Lyra

15 min east
935 W Eastland Ave, East Nashville · dinner only, Mon–Sat 5–10pm, closed Sun ★ SPLURGE

The chef-driven, modern end of the spectrum — Chef Hrant Arakelian doing a seasonal Middle Eastern menu that's genuinely one of the best meals in Nashville, in any category. Small, warm room. If you're going to have one nice dinner out this week, make it this.

Order: hummus, man'oushe, fried halloumi, baba ghanoush, and whatever the seasonal special is. Reserve ahead — it books up.

Epice

8 min
2902 12th Ave S, 12 South · Lebanese · Tue–Sat 11am–10pm, Sun to 9pm, closed Mon

Lebanese bistro on 12 South's restaurant row — closest of the good Middle Eastern options to you, and a lovely patio. Mezze-forward, bright, easy. Great for a long lunch, and you can walk 12 South's shops after.

Order: the mezze spread — muhammara, labneh, kibbeh nayeh if you're into it — plus the lamb.

Middle Eatz

15 min
412 Harding Place · Yemeni, family-owned

Widely called the best falafel in Nashville, and Yemeni food is rare here — different spicing, different breads, worth the trip on its own.

🌮 Mexican

Same road, different half. Nolensville Pike is lined with taquerías, panaderías, and carnicerías, and a lot of them are excellent. Then there are two or three destination spots elsewhere in town that are worth a special trip.

The destinations

Mas Tacos Por Favor

15 min east
732 McFerrin Ave, East Nashville · Tue–Sat 11am–9pm, Sun 10am–4pm, closed Mon ★ TOP PICK

Started in a 1974 Winnebago, now a Nashville institution and reigning winner of the Scene's Best Taco readers' poll. Scratch-made, counter-service, cash-friendly, always a little bit of a line. This is the one everybody who lives here loves.

Order: the fried avocado taco and the fish taco (people compare it to California), the sweet potato & quinoa taco, tortilla soup, and an horchata or agua fresca. Go at an off hour.

Maíz de la Vida

8 min
Near the Gulch / Pie Town · heirloom masa ★ SPLURGE

Chef Julio Hernández — James Beard Emerging Chef 2023, Best Chef: Southeast semifinalist 2025 — nixtamalizes heirloom Mexican corn in-house and hand-presses every tortilla daily. The tortilla itself is the point, and once you've had one you'll notice the difference everywhere else. This started as a food truck and is now one of the most acclaimed restaurants in the city.

Order: anything on a tortilla, plus whatever the mole or special of the day is. Check hours before you go — the dine-in schedule has changed over time.

Nolensville Pike — the real deal

Tacos y Mariscos El Amigo

15 min
3901 Nolensville Pike · in an old gas station

Exactly what it sounds like and exactly as good as that implies. Cheap, flavorful, tacos and seafood. A local favorite for years.

Mas Amigos Mexican Kitchen

12 min
2615 Nolensville Pike · bright, modern room

The comfortable one on the strip — good if you want a proper sit-down dinner with a full bar rather than a counter and plastic chairs. Food still holds up.

Taquería México

17 min
4652 Nolensville Pike

No-frills, consistently excellent. Al pastor and suadero.

Carnicería y Taquería Don Juan

15 min
Near Nolensville Pike & Thompson Ln

Butcher counter in front, taquería in back — the classic format, and usually a sign of good tacos. Order at the counter, eat at a folding table, be very happy.

Las Américas

15 min
Nolensville Pike · pan-Latin

Broader than strictly Mexican — Central and South American dishes alongside. Good for pupusas and things you won't find at the taquerías.

Also on the strip

12–18 min
If you want to just wander

El Tapatio, Las Cazuelas, La Terraza, Taquería San Luis, Los Cantaritos, and La Hacienda are all on or near Nolensville Pike and all have their partisans. Honestly, the strategy that works best here is to drive the strip, pick the place with the fullest parking lot, and go in. It's hard to do badly.

Don't skip the panaderías. Any Mexican bakery on the Pike — walk in, grab a tray and tongs, load up on conchas and pan dulce for about $1 each. Best cheap breakfast in Nashville.

🍗 Nashville Classics

You're here for a week — you should eat the three things Nashville actually invented or perfected: hot chicken, the meat-and-three, and a proper biscuit.

Prince's Hot Chicken

18 min
5814 Nolensville Pike · Mon–Sat 11am–10pm, closed Sun ★ THE ORIGINAL

The Thornton family invented Nashville hot chicken here. Deeper, darker crust, brown sugar in the paste, pepper-forward heat that builds and lingers. Order it fresh and expect to wait — it's cooked to order and that's the point.

Heat guidance: "Medium" at Prince's is hotter than "hot" almost anywhere else. If you're unsure, get mild — it still has real heat. Get white bread and pickles, potato salad, and a drink you can refill.

Hattie B's Hot Chicken — Midtown

walkable
112 19th Ave S · essentially next door to you

The modern, approachable version — brighter, tangier heat and a crisp orange-red crust. Six heat levels from "Southern" (no heat) up to "Shut the Cluck Up." Purists prefer Prince's; Hattie B's is genuinely good and it's a five-minute walk from your Airbnb. Lines can be long at peak — go at 11am or after 8pm.

Order: Medium is the sweet spot. Get the pimento mac and the black-eyed pea salad.

Arnold's Country Kitchen

7 min
605 8th Ave S · lunch only, weekdays · James Beard America's Classics ★ TOP PICK

The definitive Nashville meat-and-three: you slide a tray down a cafeteria line, pick one meat and three vegetables, and Jack Arnold's family has been doing it since 1982. Roast beef, fried green tomatoes, turnip greens, chocolate pie. Everyone from construction crews to country stars is in that line.

Critical: lunch only, and only on weekdays — typically around 10:30am to 2:30pm. Don't plan on it for dinner or a weekend. Get the roast beef and don't skip the chess or chocolate pie.

Pancake Pantry

walkable
1796 21st Ave S, Hillsboro Village · cash-friendly, expect a line

A Nashville institution since 1961, right in your neighborhood. 20-odd kinds of pancakes, sweet and savory, with house-made syrups. The line down the sidewalk is part of the experience — it moves faster than it looks.

Order: the sweet potato pancakes or the Swiss chocolate crêpes. Go on a weekday morning; weekends are brutal.

Brown's Diner

walkable
Blair Blvd, Hillsboro Village · open since 1927

One of the oldest restaurants still operating in Nashville, half of it built into an old trolley car. Cheeseburger, cold beer, songwriters at the bar. Not fancy, not trying to be, and beloved for exactly that.

🍽️ More Food Worth Crossing Town For

Nashville's restaurant scene got very good, very fast. These are the ones that would be notable in any city.

Rolf and Daughters

700 Taylor St, Germantown · pasta, dinner

The restaurant that put modern Nashville on the national map. House-made pasta in a converted factory building. Reserve.

Butcher & Bee

902 Main St, East Nashville · Mediterranean-ish

Whipped feta with hot honey is the dish people drive across town for. Great brunch, great patio, sits right in your Middle Eastern wheelhouse.

Café Roze

1115 Porter Rd, East Nashville · all-day

Pink-hued, effortlessly cool, good from 8am coffee through evening cocktails. Excellent solo-with-a-book spot.

Folk

823 Meridian St, East Nashville · wood-fired

Wood-fired pizza and vegetables, bright and seasonal. One of the most consistently pleasant meals in the city.

Nashville Farmers' Market

900 Rosa Parks Blvd · lunch daily

Farm sheds plus an indoor food hall with ~20 vendors — Jamaican jerk, Korean bibimbap, Indian biryani, vegan Mexico City tacos, Neapolitan pizza, gyros. Cheap, air-conditioned, great for a hot afternoon. Next to Bicentennial Mall.

Proper Bagel

Belmont Blvd · near you

Legitimately good bagels, a wall of flavored cream cheeses, and a short drive from the Airbnb. Good pre-hike fuel.

🎸 Music, the Way Locals Do It

Broadway — the neon honky-tonk strip downtown — is worth exactly one hour of your life, once, so you can say you did it. Everything good is elsewhere.

🎟️ The Bluebird Cafe takes planning. Nashville's famous songwriter listening room (4104 Hillsboro Pike, ~10 min from you). Reservations are online only at bluebirdcafe.com — no phone reservations — and they release on a schedule and vanish fast. There's a $15/seat food-and-drink minimum plus cover. If you miss out: for most Tuesday–Sunday shows they hold 10–12 church-pew seats for walk-ups, first-come when doors open. Get in line early. It's a genuinely quiet, respectful room — no talking during songs.

Station Inn

★ do this
402 12th Ave S, The Gulch · bluegrass

A cinderblock building the Gulch's glass towers grew up around. World-class bluegrass, cash bar, folding chairs, mostly no reservations — show up early. The most authentic music room in Nashville.

Robert's Western World

416 Broadway · free, all day

The one honky-tonk on Broadway that still has real honky-tonk bands. Fried bologna sandwich, boots on the wall, no cover. If you do Broadway, do this.

Ryman Auditorium

116 5th Ave N · the Mother Church

Best-sounding room in America, in an 1892 tabernacle. Check the calendar — if anything you'd remotely enjoy is playing, buy the ticket. Daytime self-guided tours are also excellent.

Rudy's Jazz Room

Wedgewood-Houston · jazz

Basement jazz club, New Orleans food, low ceilings, great players. Reserve a table.

The Basement East

East Nashville · rock/indie

Where East Nashville sees bands. Check who's on — it's usually someone good.

3rd & Lindsley

Rock, soul, blues

Long-running room with a great house sound. The Monday night Time Jumpers residency (Vince Gill's western swing band) is a Nashville rite of passage — check if it's running.

Grand Ole Opry

Opry House, Music Valley

Touristy, yes — also genuinely fun and unlike anything else. Five or six acts a night, live radio broadcast with the ads read on stage, huge range of performers.

Santa's Pub

2225 Bransford Ave · karaoke in a trailer

A double-wide trailer painted Christmas red, run by a man in a Santa beard, cash only, $2 beer, and the best karaoke in the South. Everyone from record execs to bikers. A true Nashville institution.

🎶 Free live music, most evenings. Musicians Corner in Centennial Park — a walk from your Airbnb — runs free outdoor shows on select dates, with food trucks and local artisans. Check conservancyonline.com for what's on during your week. Cheekwood's "Songwriters Under the Stars" is another summer evening option: bring a blanket, buy wine, sit in the gardens.

🏛️ For the Hot Part of the Day

Save these for 12–4pm when it's 100° outside, or for the afternoon a thunderstorm rolls through.

The Parthenon & Centennial Park

walkable
2500 West End Ave · park free, museum ticketed

A full-scale replica of the Athenian Parthenon with a 42-foot gilded Athena inside, plus art galleries. Absurd and wonderful. The 132-acre park around it has a sunken garden, a lake, and walking trails, and it's open dawn to 11pm. This is your closest green space — great for an evening walk.

Cheekwood Estate & Gardens

1200 Forrest Park Dr · ~15 min

A 1930s limestone mansion on 55 acres of formal gardens with a sculpture trail through the woods. Given how much you like being outside, this is the best hot-day compromise: shaded garden paths, a real art museum indoors, and a café. Go late afternoon when the light is good and the heat breaks.

Frist Art Museum

919 Broadway · ~7 min

A gorgeous 1934 art deco former post office, now a rotating-exhibition museum. No permanent collection, so check what's up — but the building alone is worth it, and it's blessedly cold inside.

Country Music Hall of Fame

Downtown

Even if country isn't your thing, it's an extremely well-made museum about American music generally. Two to three hours.

Third Man Records

623 7th Ave S

Jack White's label, storefront, and record-pressing operation. You can watch records get pressed through a window, and there's a booth where you can cut your own vinyl. Weird and great, 20 minutes.

Grimey's Records

1060 E Trinity Ln, East Nashville

The great Nashville record store, in an old church. Frequent free in-store performances — check the calendar.

Bicentennial Capitol Mall

Next to the Farmers' Market

An outdoor park that's essentially a walk-through map of Tennessee — a 200-foot granite state map, a river wall, a 95-bell carillon. Combine with lunch at the Farmers' Market.

Belle Meade Historic Site

~15 min, near Percy Warner

A 19th-century thoroughbred farm with an honest, well-done tour that includes the enslaved people who ran it. Winery tasting included. Easy to pair with a Percy Warner morning.

💡 Practical Notes

Before you go

  • Buy your Cummins Falls gorge permit as soon as you know the date — $7, online only, capped daily, sells out.
  • Check the Bluebird Cafe calendar the day reservations drop for your week.
  • Check the Ryman calendar and Musicians Corner (Centennial Park) for your dates.
  • Reserve Lyra and Rolf and Daughters if you want them — they book up.

Hiking in Tennessee in August

  • Be at the trailhead by 7–8am. By 11 it's genuinely unpleasant, and by 2 it's a bad idea.
  • Carry more water than you think — roughly a liter per hour of hiking in this heat.
  • Ticks and chiggers are real here. Permethrin on shoes and socks, DEET on skin, and check yourself after. Not a scare tactic; it's just what people do.
  • There are copperheads in Middle Tennessee. Watch where you put hands and feet on rocks, and they'll leave you alone.
  • Poison ivy is everywhere off-trail. "Leaves of three."
  • Afternoon thunderstorms build fast. If you hear thunder on a ridge, head down.
  • Never enter a gorge or creek bed when rain is forecast anywhere upstream. Flash flooding is the actual danger in this terrain, not the heat.

Getting around

  • Parking is free and easy outside downtown and The Gulch. Downtown, use a garage — street parking is a hassle.
  • Nashville drivers are not aggressive, but I-440 and I-65 back up 4–6pm.
  • Most of Nolensville Pike is 15–20 minutes from you and easy to park at.

Small things that make it better

  • Cash helps at a few places — Santa's Pub, some taquerías, some counters.
  • Many of the best spots on Nolensville Pike are closed Sunday or Monday. Check before driving out.
  • Arnold's is weekday lunch only. It's the most common mistake visitors make.
  • Tennessee has no state income tax, but sales tax is high (~9.25%) — prices ring up above the menu.
  • Nashville is friendly. If you're eating alone at a counter, someone will talk to you. That's normal here.

Have a great week, Lindsey.
